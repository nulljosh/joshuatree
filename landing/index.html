Meet Samantha.

Boot straight into her. Type what you want, and she runs the machine.

Type what you want and she opens an app, sets a reminder, jots a note, or checks the weather. She opens with her eyes open and talks back with her portrait on the dock.

Samantha comes from Turing, a separate project. Joshua Tree is the machine she runs.

Everything you need is already there.

A full set of apps, built from nothing but a screen and a keyboard.

One app crashes. The desktop stays up.

Each app runs as its own protected program, walled off from the kernel and from every other app. 38 of the 38 run that way now. Every app is out of the kernel.

Now on a real Raspberry Pi.

The same desktop, on a small board you can hold. One command puts it on an SD card. Here is what works on a Pi 4 today, and what does not yet.

The whole desktop. Wallpaper, menu bar, dock and windows, full screen at 1080p. It boots to a screen with the tree and a progress bar.

Apps that cannot hurt it. Calendar, Notes and Music run in user mode, walled off from the kernel. If one crashes, the desktop carries on.

It keeps your files. The Pi reads and writes its own SD card. Notes saves two seconds after your last key, and the note is still there after you pull the power.

A Bluetooth mouse. It scans, pairs and moves the pointer, so there is no cable to find. USB keyboards and mice work any time, with Caps Lock and key repeat. The first test on a real Bluetooth mouse is the next step.

Sound and your music. Volume keys, a beep, and a Music app that plays the MP3s you own, with their titles, artists and covers. Apple Music downloads are out; their files are locked.

Samantha, on the board. Type a question and she answers from Turing, our own model, straight from Turing's own site. No key and no relay. Talking to her out loud still goes through a small relay on your Mac.

Wi-Fi, done by hand. The Pi's radio chip cannot join a protected network on its own, so Joshua Tree does the WPA2 handshake itself, gets an address and sets its clock from the internet.

Still ahead. Many windows at once, and the rest of the desktop apps on the Pi. See the roadmap.

Kernel
A freestanding kernel for PCs (i386) and the Raspberry Pi 4 (ARM64). No libc, and no dependencies beyond the toolchain.
